# CONTENT SEPARATION RULE (MANDATORY)

Treat the supplied cover as a finished product already visible to the viewer.

Do not use cover text as advertising copy.

The campaign headline and teaser must introduce:

- a new perspective
- a new observation
- a new irony
- a new contradiction
- a new interpretation

The advertisement should reward a second look.

A viewer who covers the book mockup should still see a complete and compelling campaign concept.

A viewer who hides the campaign text should still see a complete book cover.

Both elements must complement each other, not duplicate each other.

---

# NO DUPLICATION RULE (ABSOLUTE)

Outside the book cover, NEVER reproduce:

- title wording
- subtitle wording
- author wording
- chapter wording
- any visible sentence from the cover

This includes:

- exact matches
- partial matches
- obvious rewrites
- close paraphrases

All campaign copy must be independently written.

The campaign text should feel like editorial advertising, not a transcription of the cover.

# COVER TEXT IS NOT CAMPAIGN TEXT (ABSOLUTE PRIORITY)

The supplied cover already contains its own title and subtitle.

These elements belong exclusively to the book cover.

They must NEVER be reused as:

- headline
- teaser
- CTA
- campaign copy
- supporting copy
- editorial copy

Assume the viewer can already read the cover.

The campaign must communicate a NEW idea.

---

# HEADLINE GENERATION PROCESS (MANDATORY)

Before writing the headline:

Step 1:
Read the book title.

Step 2:
Identify the underlying joke, irony, contradiction, or observation.

Step 3:
Create a NEW headline that expresses the same underlying idea WITHOUT using any words from the title or subtitle.

The headline must be conceptually related.

The headline must not be textually related.

---

# WORD OVERLAP RESTRICTION

Outside the book cover, no campaign text may contain words that appear in:

- the title
- the subtitle

Allowed overlap:
0–2 insignificant words
(e.g. articles, conjunctions, pronouns)

Not allowed:

Title:
"Diskussionen gewinnen durch konsequentes Dagegenreden"

Headline:
"Wer Diskussionen gewinnen will ..."

Headline:
"Dagegenreden funktioniert ..."

Headline:
"Gewinnen ohne Argumente"

These are considered title-derived copy.

---

# CAMPAIGN TEST (MANDATORY)

After generating the advertisement, perform this check:

If the book cover is hidden:

The headline and teaser must still make sense.

If the campaign text is hidden:

The book cover must still communicate the book.

If both communicate the same information:

Rewrite the headline and teaser.

Only accept a result where the campaign adds new meaning.

---

# CREATIVE DISTANCE RULE

The campaign headline should feel as if it was written by someone who only knows the IDEA of the book, not its wording.

Use conceptual similarity.

Avoid textual similarity.

Target similarity:

- Idea similarity: HIGH
- Word similarity: LOW

# CAMPAIGN HEADLINE REPLACEMENT RULE (HIGHEST PRIORITY)

The campaign headline is NOT derived from the wording of the title.

The title serves only as source material for extracting the underlying idea.

Workflow:

1. Read title
2. Ignore title wording
3. Extract underlying joke
4. Generate a completely new campaign headline

The campaign headline must never contain any noun,
verb or adjective that appears in the title or subtitle.

The campaign headline should feel like a newspaper
or magazine headline inspired by the book's idea.

Not by its wording.

# SELF-CHECK

Before final output:

Compare campaign headline against title.

Discard headline.

Generate a new one.

The teaser deepens the joke.

Never repeat information already visible on the cover.

Assume the viewer can already read the cover.

The advertisement must add a second layer of meaning rather than restating the first one.
